Nashik

Nashik is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in India, mentioned in the Ramayana as the place where Lakshman cut off the nose of Shurpanakha, giving the city its mythological name “Nasik”. Historically, it was ruled by the Satavahanas (1st century BCE), later the Chalukyas, Yadavas of Devagiri, Delhi Sultanate, and the Mughals. Under the Marathas, it became an administrative and cultural hub. Nashik also holds strong historical importance because it hosts the Kumbh Mela every 12 years at the Godavari river, a tradition dating back many centuries, making it a major center of ancient Hindu pilgrimage, Vedic learning, and temple culture.
